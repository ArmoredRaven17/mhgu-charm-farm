Other MHGU Apps

Companion fan tools by the same author. Each opens in a new tab.

  • Quest Randomizer Rolls random quests when you can't decide what to hunt.
  • Collection Tracker Tracks which weapons, armor and Palico gear you own, and what it costs to finish them.
  • Equipment Box Mirrors the in-game box — 2000 hunter slots and 1000 Palico — so you can plan where things sit.
  • Bingo Builds a bingo card of hunting goals, with a shareable seed so a group can race the same board.
  • Hunting Log Keeps a running record of your hunts — what you were after, what you wore, who came along.
  • Zenny Gauntlet A scored challenge run — fail a hunt or get carted and you lose that weapon/style combo for the rest of the run.
  • Set Builder Assemble a full set by hand and see exactly which skills it activates.
  • Weapon Trees Browse every weapon upgrade tree, with full stats on any node you click.
  • Challenge Run A permadeath challenge run — start from a root weapon, lose it on any quest failure, clear every Key Quest.
  • Charm Farm you are here Beat charms out of a Brachydios, then meld three of a rarity into one fresh roll.

Controls

The hunt
Click the monster
Deal damage. At zero HP it drops charms, ore and zenny
Space
Same as clicking — hold it down
Smithy
Spend zenny and ore on upgrades. Ore comes from the variant wearing that ore's colours
Palico vs hunter
A hired Palico adds flat damage per second. A Hunter for Hire throws a real attack instead — it uses your click damage and can crit, so hunters get better the more you've spent on hitting harder
Ore chip
Click to sell that ore at the game's real price
God charm
A Creator Talisman — rarity 10 — with three slots and both skills at the top of their range. Nothing below rarity 10 counts, however it rolls. It glows gold in the box, Sell Junk will never take one, and it can't be fed to the pot — melding one away could only ever be a mistake
Junk ≤
Sets the highest rarity Sell Junk will clear out
The hires
Maximeld XIV reloads the Melding Pot after every hunt. Neko (Means Cat) sells your junk charms, using whatever Junk ≤ is set to — handy because low rarities you never hold three of can't reach the pot at all. The Argosy Captain sells off ore that no remaining upgrade will ever ask for, and trims the surplus of the ones still wanted. Kokoto Gal spends your zenny and ore on upgrades. All four are one-off hires costing a stack of G-rank ore, and between them the farm runs itself
Away from the tab
Palicoes and hired hunters keep working while the page is closed, up to 8 hours' worth. You're told what they caught when you come back
The box and the pot
Click a charm
Inspect it
Drag to the pot
Load a charm into a melding slot
Click a pot slot
Send that charm back to the box
Melding
Three charms of the same rarity become one charm of the next rarity up — three rarity 1s make a rarity 2. Rarity 10 is the ceiling, so three 10s return a 10, which is still a fresh roll on the best table. As in the game, one meld resolves per hunt — load the pot, then go killing. Whatever has been waiting longest goes first, so a row you just filled never jumps ahead of one already on the stack. Each row shows how many hunts away its turn is
Auto-fill
Loads rows from whatever you have three-of, best rarity first
Ctrl + S
Save to a file
Esc
Close a dialog
The roster

Every Brachydios below the Raging one is the same sprite wearing an ore's colours. Which ones can show up depends on your rank, matching the ranks that ore can actually be mined at in the game.

About / Attributions

An unofficial, non-commercial fan toy: grind a Brachydios for talismans, then feed three of a rarity into the Melding Pot to get one of the next rarity up. The charms it rolls are real — every skill and point value comes from the game's own charm tables, so nothing it produces is something MHGU couldn't give you.

Development: Built by the author with substantial help from Claude Code, Anthropic's AI coding tool. Most of the code, the data-extraction scripts and this page were written with AI assistance, reviewed and directed by the author.

Charm tables, ore names, prices and mining ranks: derived from Kiranico and the community MHGU database.

English item, skill and equipment names: MHGUDB-iOS (MIT) & gatheringhallstudios community database. The game files store names in Japanese only, so the English naming throughout this tool comes from their work.
